Programs

The starter kit for the things nobody hands you.

Fourteen lessons in four tracks. A caseworker would call the first one "financial literacy". We call it the $500 card, because that is what it is actually about and that is what someone coming home would search for.

How the consultation works

Every lesson is dated. Undated advice is a claim about right now that nobody checked.

Two programs, one foundation.

The Better Neighbors work rests on three pillars: art, reentry support and education, and community. Art Inside Out is the flagship and has its own home. The fourteen lessons below are the second pillar, rebuilt by people who have actually walked out of a facility and had to make the first year work.

They are not competing programs. One teaches a person how to read their own mistakes with a pencil. The other teaches them how to read a text message that is trying to take their money. Both are needed and neither replaces the other.

Foundation, flagship

Art Inside Out

Kirk Charlton's twelve lesson method, built inside Eastern Oregon Correctional Institution. You do not erase the wrong line, you read it.

Foundation, second pillar

The fourteen lessons

Practical, current and specific. The world you are walking into, described by people who walked into it themselves.

Track one

The device in your hand.

The single biggest gap, and the one that produces the most anxiety in the first month. The phone is not a neutral tool. It was designed to hold attention, and it is also the main way people coming home get targeted.

Smartphone practice and anxiety

Your first phone in a decade

Setting it up, keeping it quiet, and building a relationship with it that does not spike a nervous system already running hot. Notifications off is a skill, not a preference.

Scam and fraud awareness

Who is actually texting you

New number, new accounts, thin history and a real wish for something to go right. That profile is targeted deliberately. We teach the patterns rather than a list of current scams, because the list changes monthly.

Account security and recovery

The password problem

Why the same password everywhere is the vulnerability, what a manager actually does, and how to set up recovery so a lost phone is an inconvenience instead of a catastrophe.

AI literacy

Using AI without getting burned

It is genuinely useful and it confidently makes things up. Where it helps, where it will embarrass you, and what you should never put into it.

Social media and attention

The feed is not your friend

The pressure to be online is a farce, and for this population it is closer to a liability than a lifeline. What it costs, what it risks, and what to do instead.

Track two

Money that holds.

Starting from zero has a specific path. It is not obvious, it is not fast, and almost every shortcut offered to someone in the first year out is a trap wearing a suit.

Secured credit and credit building

The $500 card

The small limit secured card is the standard on ramp when you have no file at all. How it is scored, what to put on it, and the mistakes that quietly undo a year of work.

Budgeting that survives a bad week

Money that lasts past Friday

Systems, not willpower. The first money goes before it lands and nobody warns you about that gap. We build the structure before the paycheck arrives.

Schemes, MLMs and recruitment pitches

It is not a business, it is a pyramid

Someone will approach you with an opportunity in your first six months. How to recognise the shape of it, and why this population is a deliberate target for it.

Track three

Living under conditions.

This is the track no outside organisation can write. It comes from years of real interactions with supervision officers, employers, property managers, people you would like to date, and the general public.

Supervision and officer interaction

Sitting across from your PO

How to prepare, what to bring, what to document and how to raise a request. Not gaming anyone. Being clear, calm and on the record in a conversation that carries real weight.

Mindset under restriction

What you have, not what you lost

The restrictions are real. Staring only at the column you cannot change is its own sentence. How to hold both lists honestly without one erasing the other.

Adult logistics after institutional life

Nobody is feeding you anymore

Years of having every basic necessity provided is not freedom, and leaving it is not simply relief. Meals, appointments, supplies, laundry, transport and time all become yours at once.

This track especially: written by someone who has been through it.

Track four

The person you are now.

Most people leaving incarceration have been through abuse, suffering and harm that is hard to describe to anyone who was not there, and they kept moving anyway. Understanding why you struggle and excel where you do changes what you expect of yourself.

CPTSD, ADHD, AuDHD and trauma

Why your brain does that

Your wiring and your history are not separate topics. Where you struggle and where you are unusually capable usually trace to the same root, and knowing that is a relief rather than a diagnosis to carry.

Mental health access

Getting a therapist who gets it

How to find one, what to ask in the first call, what medication conversations actually look like, and how to tell early whether someone is equipped for this history.

Relationships, disclosure and rejection

Dating with a record

When to tell someone, how, and what happens next. Rejection is a likely outcome and pretending otherwise sets people up. So does letting it become the reason to stop trying.

Five messages. Three are scams.

This is the actual teaching method. Not a list of scams to memorise, which goes stale in weeks, but the shape they share.

USPS: your parcel could not be delivered, incomplete address. Update it here: usps-redelivery-track.com

Hey is this you? Got your number from a mutual friend. I mentor people building side income and I think you would be good at this. You open?

Your bank: we declined a $340 charge on your card. If this was not you, no action is needed. Questions, call the number on the back of your card.

Congratulations! You are pre-approved for a $5,000 personal loan. No credit check required. Reply YES to claim.

Reminder of your appointment Thursday at 2pm. To confirm or reschedule, use the office number printed on your own paperwork.

The rule underneath all five: does it send you somewhere you already trust, or somewhere it chose for you. Learn the shape and you do not need the list.

One to one, not a seat in a classroom.
