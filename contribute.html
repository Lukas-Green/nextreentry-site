A call for transition stories

What you learned the hard way is the resource.

This curriculum is not finished and it never will be. It gets sharper every time somebody who has come home tells us what was hardest, what actually helped, and what nobody warned them about. You can do this anonymously.

Go to the three questions

Three questions, no name required, and nothing published without your specific permission.

One person's bad month becomes the next person's warning.

Every organisation in this sector says it is evidence based. Most of them mean somebody else's study. The evidence we care about is what actually happened to people who walked out and had to work it out.

When enough people tell us the same thing went wrong at the same point, that is not an anecdote anymore. That is a gap in the map, and we build a lesson over it.

This is also how we make sure the material is not written only for the majority. If the experience of women coming home is missing from what we collect, the curriculum will quietly be written for men. We would rather ask.

Every lesson we build from these carries the mark: written by someone who has been through it.
What happens to it

It becomes curriculum, not content

We are not collecting stories to put moving quotes on a fundraising page. We are collecting them to find where the existing advice fails.

Who reads it

The people writing the lessons

All of whom have been incarcerated. You are not explaining this to someone who needs the basics explained.

What we promise about your words.

01

Anonymous means anonymous

Leave the box ticked and we never ask who you are, and we never open your email app, because your own address would be sitting at the top of it. We hand you your words instead and you send them from wherever you like. We only ever see what you wrote.

02

Nothing is published without a separate yes

Sending this is not permission to quote you. Publishing is a different question and we ask it separately, in writing, afterwards.

03

You can withdraw it

Change your mind at any point and it comes out. You do not have to give a reason and we will not ask for one.

04

We do not want case details

We are not asking what you were convicted of. It is not relevant to what we are building and we would rather not hold it.

05

Leave other people out

Please do not name anyone else, including officers, employers or family. Their consent is not yours to give.

06

This is not a crisis line

Nobody monitors this in real time. If you are in danger or in crisis right now, contact local emergency services or a crisis line rather than this form.

Three questions.

Write as much or as little as you want. One honest sentence in the right place is worth more to us than a polished page.

How you want to send this
Publishing

This is a separate question from sending it. Whatever you pick, we can still learn from what you wrote.

A story is not the only thing worth handing over.

Correct us

If a lesson on this site is out of date or wrong for your county, that is the most useful message we can receive. Say so.

Name a resource that worked

A clinic, an employer, a landlord, a class. Real things that actually took someone with a record.

Teach a lesson

If you came home and made it work, there is a track here with your name on it.
